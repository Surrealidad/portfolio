    How to read the KPIs

    SPI, schedule performance: budget of the work done divided by the budget of the work planned by now. Below 1 means behind.

    Schedule variance in weeks: how far the work done is from where the plan expected it, in weeks (earned schedule). Negative means behind.

    CPI, cost performance: budget of the work done divided by what it actually cost. Below 1 means over budget. QA weeks with nothing to test count as cost with no work done.

    Forecast cost: actual cost so far, plus the remaining work at its planned cost adjusted by each territory and stage's CPI so far, plus QA team weeks until the forecast delivery.

    Weeks late per stage: forecast end of the batch the stage is working on, against its planned end. It includes delay inherited from earlier stages.

    Weeks lost per stage: actual minus planned durations. It only counts delay the stage created itself, which is what points at the vendor or team to talk to.

    Flag thresholds

    !Watch 1 week lost in a stage, CPI below 0.95, or 3 QA team weeks idle.

    !At risk 2 weeks lost, CPI below 0.90, a QA batch held up, or delivery forecast 1 week after the plan.

    ✕Critical 4 weeks lost or more, CPI below 0.85, or delivery 2 or more weeks after the plan.

    ✓Ahead At least 2 weeks gained in a stage, or ahead of plan overall.

    Planning assumptions

    Duration = base weeks × (files ÷ 5,000)e, rounded up to whole weeks, minimum one week. An exponent below 1 means larger batches are handled more efficiently.

    How the plan works

    Content Management runs once, in the source language, for all 40,000 files. Each batch it finishes is released to the 8 territories at the same time.

    Translation and Recording are external vendors, one per territory, and each territory moves on as soon as its own batch is ready.

    Mastering is a single in-house team with capacity for all 8 territories. It takes a batch for a territory as soon as that territory has finished recording it, without waiting for the others.

    QA is one in-house tester team per territory. The teams are formed together and billed by the week. A batch goes to QA only once it has been mastered for all 8 territories, so a late territory holds up QA for everyone, and the waiting is paid for.
